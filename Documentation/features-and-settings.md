# Features and settings

This guide describes features currently represented in the source. Hardware-dependent behavior can be unavailable, and provider-backed features depend on the connected account. An idea absent from this page may be planned; see [Roadmap](roadmap.md).

## Companion and conversation

**Yami, the cat spirit.** Yami is a floating, armless spirit with cat ears and a round, front-facing face whose lower half burns downward as a layered flame: a pale outer flame, a lighter middle and a white-hot core (`src/yami/`). While she is hovered, listening, thinking, speaking or being carried, the flame tongues flicker and curl, embers drift off and fade, and a soft glow under her shows she is floating. Afterwards she settles to a still pose and stops requesting animation frames. Turn on **Living flame** to keep a gentle flicker at idle. Reduced motion shows static poses.

**Spirit island.** A small top-center transparent island shows Yami and her listening/thinking/speaking state. Click anywhere on the notch (or scroll down on it) to open the drawer, double-click to open the full app, and right-click to arm an equipped ability. After about eight quiet seconds the notch slims to a thin bar and Yami tucks away; hovering it, a voice turn or any activity brings her back. Opening the drawer grows it out of the notch and hides Yami; closing plays the reverse. Messages that do not fit the collapsed island, such as a missing API key, appear in the caption pill at the bottom of the screen. While Yami runs a tool, a small card under the island names the action (for example "Opening Spotify"). When media is playing, hovering the small now-playing pill grows it into a card with progress and previous/play/next controls. Hovering the island otherwise hints that scrolling opens more. Scrolling down on the island opens the drawer; scrolling up at the top of the drawer tucks it away. It remains in the Windows tray when the main window is closed. Quit Yami from the tray to stop background work.

**Roaming.** With **Let Yami roam** on (Settings > Behavior, or the tray menu), Yami floats in a small click-through window on the desktop. She dodges the cursor twice with a quip, then lets you catch her: click opens Yami, drag moves her. She drifts to a nearby spot every few minutes (skipped on battery or while a fullscreen app is in front), hides while a fullscreen game, video or presentation is in front, naps after a minute alone, and flies into the island during voice and dictation turns. The tray checkbox follows the Settings toggle.

**Captions.** During a voice or dictation turn a pill at the bottom of the screen shows the held shortcut keys, your live transcript (`You`), then Yami's reply (`Yami`). The caption window exists only for the turn and closes a few seconds after it ends.

**Text chat.** Home sends text to Gemini’s text model. Up to the latest eight messages are included in the system prompt and up to 16 messages are used to construct request history. The turn can invoke declared tools, with a maximum of eight tool-call rounds. If no Gemini key is configured, chat is unavailable while local utilities remain available.

**Live voice.** Hold the voice shortcut or press the island ability button, speak, then release to finish. Voice connects through the Gemini Live API and streams audio. Yami uses explicit speech activity start/end boundaries; it does not send continuously when idle. A voice turn can make the same declared tool calls as text chat. A lasso selection with Gemini connected starts a one-shot voice question about the selected image.

**Dictation.** Hold the dictation shortcut. Yami remembers the foreground window handle, captures speech, asks Gemini to transcribe and resolve spoken corrections, then inserts the final text into that original field using clipboard-preserving paste. Password fields are rejected by the native host. Dictation text is also retained in conversation history. The engine is chosen in AI > Dictation: Gemini (the default); an installed Windows speech recognizer; or **Whistle offline**, Cactus Compute's 16.9 MB Whistle model run by its 1.6 MB Needle engine on this PC's processor (English, German, French, Spanish, Italian, Dutch and Polish). Both offline engines transcribe 16 kHz mono WAV locally and need no key. Choosing Whistle turns on the Offline dictation module and downloads its two pinned files; dictionary rules still apply afterwards. Whistle was verified on Windows on 2026-10-28: Yami runs it with English forced and Your words as keywords (flags from the pinned runner's `--help`), and a synthesized sentence dictated into Yami's own chat box in about 1 second.

**Circle to ask.** Hold the lasso shortcut, draw a loop around anything on screen, and complete the selection. Capture occurs locally. With Gemini connected, the selected image can be sent with a voice turn; without a key, the image appears in the console selection. Cancel with Escape.

**Embers, skins, voices, personalities, and abilities.** The loadout presents cosmetic options and unlock prices for Yami. The current implementation awards an ember per completed voice turn or dictation. Soul, voice, personality, and ability choices are saved locally; they do not represent provider billing or a verified recreation of an original reward system. Paid voice cards use Gemini prebuilt voices and delivery prompts; they are not separately trained voices. The custom personality editor appears after that option is owned.

| Slot | Shipped choices and ember costs |
| --- | --- |
| Skin | Yami free; Ember 160; Wisp 160; Amethyst 400; Gilded 400; Void 1,000. Each is the same cat spirit with its own flame colours. Saved `hitodama` selections from earlier builds migrate to `yami` on start. |
| Voice | Charon free; Puck, Leda, Aoede, Enceladus, Algenib, Laomedeia, Jack, Ankit, Cheng, and Linda cost 240 each. Several named personas map to Gemini prebuilt voices plus delivery instructions. |

When an ability is equipped, the loadout’s **Trigger button** row captures a keyboard key or one of the primary mouse buttons. Escape cancels capture. Right-click Yami in the island while idle to arm the ability (a notice confirms it); the next captured trigger fires it and disarms it.

## Research and action tools

With Tavily connected, a quick search uses one basic search request. Deep research is opt-in per request, asks Gemini for two complementary queries, makes three advanced searches, and may extract source content for up to five results. Sources are shown with the answer. Actual credits are controlled by Tavily; the implementation’s expected count is detailed in [Security and provider data flow](security-and-providers.md). Without Tavily, search falls back to Wikipedia and returns up to five snippets.

Gemini can use the declared tool set described in [Architecture](architecture.md) and [Security and provider data flow](security-and-providers.md): launch a Windows app, open HTTP/HTTPS URL, open supported site search, inspect windows, focus/move/close a window, read accessible screen controls, activate an accessible control, click a raw coordinate, insert text, change volume/brightness/theme, control media, manage to-dos, schedule allowed work, save a memory, read/list files, write/move/recycle a file, or run PowerShell when enabled. Confirmations and restrictions are applied by the Electron main process.

## Productivity and organization

**Today.** A clock card, a weather card (its **Turn on weather** button enables weather right there), a week strip with dots on days that have scheduled plans (pick a day to list them), a focus timer, and the to-do list. The focus timer offers 1, 5, 10, 15 and 30 minute presets; the main process owns the countdown, so it finishes with a Windows notification even when the panel is closed.

**Plans.** Save a one-off or repeating reminder, app launch, URL open, site search, or routine of several app launches. Repeat options are once, daily, and weekly. Routines can have one to ten allowlisted actions. Yami checks due work while running, including while minimized to the tray, and checks missed work when it resumes. A waitable timer requests that Windows wake a sleeping PC for the next due time. This is subject to Windows policy, firmware, hardware, and session availability.

**Memory.** Create, edit, or delete explicit facts. Memory is local; when the memory setting is on, saved facts enter Gemini’s prompt. A user may turn memory off without deleting stored entries. The local-storage disclosure is centered beneath the memory list, including when empty.

**Main navigation.** Switching among Home, Bond, Soul, Desk, and Settings slides the dock's selected pill and briefly fades in the incoming page. Reduced motion, whether set in Yami or Windows, makes these changes immediate.

**Launcher.** `Win + A` searches installed apps, local files, and saved snippets. Favorite/recent apps are local. A chosen app launches through the Windows shell. Snippets can be stored, copied, and removed.

**Clipboard.** When enabled, Yami polls for text and keeps up to 500 entries. Choose a retention period of 1, 7, 14, or 30 days. Pinned entries do not expire automatically; they are stored unencrypted in `data.json`, while unpinned entries are encrypted. Copy, pin, delete, clear, and search history from the utility panel. Each entry is a card with its time and a pin button; pinned entries are listed first. See [security limitations](security-and-providers.md#practical-security-limits) before relying on exclusions.

## File wheel

Dragging a file and tapping a modifier can open a wheel of conversions, tools, or file actions at the pointer. The same wheel is available from an Explorer shortcut (`Ctrl+Alt+Y` by default), a Send to shortcut, and a tray drop shelf. The wheel window exists only while that gesture is open.

Dropping on a conversion petal saves a new file beside the original. Images, audio, and video need the matching engine. Documents, sheets, subtitles, and zip, tar, and gz archives convert inside Yami. Slides and page pictures need LibreOffice to already be installed. Compress, details, background, the image tools, the media tools, PDF merge, split, and organize open a small window. OCR is not connected. Copy path, move, the Windows clipboard copy, PowerShell, and the float shelf are on the actions wheel. Engines download on demand into local app data after a checksum check. See [File wheel](file-wheel.md) for which paths were executed, which are plans only, and what still has to be tried on Windows.

## Launcher (Win+A)

The Apps tab of the island drawer is also a small command palette. As you type, a **Yami** group appears above apps and files:
- **Sums** such as `12*7+0.5` or `=(2+3)^2`, using + − × ÷ % ^ and brackets. Press Enter to copy the result. The calculator parses the text itself; nothing is evaluated as code.
- **Pages:** Home, Bond, Soul, Desk, AI workspace, Tune, Settings and Choose features, matched by name or by words such as "modules", "tweaks" or "inbox".
- **Module actions:** the file wheel's drop shelf and Yami Terminal.
- **Projects** from the AI workspace, which open a terminal there. The list is read without loading the workspace.

Results follow your modules, so Tune and AI appear only while they are on. **Enter** opens the first result (a Yami item, else the first app, else the first file). **Units:** `5 km to mi`, `72 f in c`, `2 gb to mb`, `3 cups in ml`, `90 min to h` or `100 km/h to mph` (length, weight, volume, data, time, speed and temperature; Enter copies the answer). **Web:** a typed address such as `github.com/xtermjs`, or a `!bang` such as `!w Kyoto`, opens straight away, in Yami Browser when that module is on and otherwise in your browser; when nothing else matches, the last row searches the web. **Agents:** an installed agent's name followed by a project, such as `claude yami`, starts that agent in a Yami Terminal tab in the project. **Files:** when Yami's own file index has fewer than ten matches for a name, it also asks Windows Search (the index Explorer uses) for files under your user folder, newest first, through a short PowerShell query with quotes and wildcards removed; the launcher can open only files it listed. A Windows Search query took about 1 second on the test PC on 2026-10-28.

## Music and media

The Controls card and the island's media peek act on the playing app's own Windows media session: play and pause, previous and next, and, when the app offers them, **seek** (drag the progress line), **shuffle** and **repeat** (off, playlist, track) as two small toggles in the time row. The Spotify desktop app, browsers and most players expose this session, so it needs no Spotify account or API key. When a session refuses play, pause or skip, Yami falls back to the media keys. Seek, shuffle and repeat appear only when the player reports them. On 2026-10-28, `scripts/verify-media.mjs` checked play/pause, next and seek against a real Windows media session: a silent test page in the Yami Browser, acted on only while it was the current session. Shuffle and repeat were tried on 2026-10-28 against the Spotify desktop app: it tells Windows it supports both and accepts the change, but does not apply it. Yami now checks the result and says so ("Spotify accepted the shuffle change but did not apply it") instead of showing a button that does nothing, and when the optional Spotify connection is on, the same buttons go through Spotify's own service (`/me/player/shuffle` and `/me/player/repeat`, which need Premium). Settings › Music has shuffle and repeat buttons too. Media actions now go to the player Yami showed, by its app, rather than whichever became Windows' current player in the meantime: during that day's checks, pausing a test page had made Spotify current and a second press paused Spotify, which was then resumed. **Lyrics** (off by default, Settings › Privacy) show the current line of a playing song in the island's media card, in the glow colour under the artist. Yami asks LRCLIB (lrclib.net), a free lyrics database with no key, once per track, sending only the artist, title and length; answers, including "no lyrics", are kept for the session. LRCLIB answered HTTP 503 during the 2026-10-28 check, so a live lookup is **not verified**; parsing and lookup are unit-tested. **Spotify (optional, Settings › Music)** adds what the Windows media session cannot: choosing which Spotify device plays (your phone, a speaker or this PC) and its volume, plus play and pause. You bring your own free Spotify developer app: create it, add the redirect URI `http://127.0.0.1:47823/callback`, add your account under User Management, and paste its Client ID. Yami signs in with PKCE, so no client secret exists. A one-off listener on `127.0.0.1:47823` takes the redirect for up to five minutes, and the refresh token is kept DPAPI-encrypted. Spotify requires Premium for these controls, and since February 2026 an app in development mode allows at most five users. Sign-in, token refresh and plain-language errors (Premium required, no active device) are unit-tested against a stand-in for Spotify; a **real Spotify account was not used**. **App volume.** Under the Controls card's volume slider, **App volume** lists apps with an audio session on the default output (playing ones in brighter text), each with its own volume slider and mute button. It uses Core Audio sessions in the native host (`native/AudioSessions.cs`) and is read only when you open it. Setting volume and mute was checked on Windows on Yami's own audio session, then restored. **Play sound through** (Controls card, shown when there is more than one output) lists the active speakers and headphones from Core Audio and makes the one you pick the default for media, games and calls. Windows has no documented call for that last step, so Yami uses `IPolicyConfig`, the undocumented interface the Sound control panel itself uses (as EarTrumpet and SoundSwitch do); only a connected device's id is accepted. On 2026-10-28 `scripts/verify-audio-output.mjs` switched this PC's default to another of its eight outputs for about a second and back.

## Look and controls

Yami uses one visual system, Nightglow: indigo glass cards over a still night sky,
with lavender reserved for what is selected or switched on. The island notch and
its drawer are opaque, because a transparent window cannot blur what is behind it.
The [design system](reference/design-system.md) lists the tokens and components.

- **Menus.** Choices on shipped screens use Yami's own menu instead of the native
  list. Arrow keys and Home/End move through it, Enter or Space chooses, letters
  jump to a match, and Escape or Tab closes it. Focus stays on the menu button.
- **Tooltips.** Icon buttons and dock items show a short Yami tooltip after about
  0.4 seconds of hover, or 0.25 seconds of keyboard focus. The native Windows
  tooltip no longer appears. Tooltips add detail; every control keeps its label.
- **Status words.** Agent, connection and usage states are a word with a coloured
  dot. The colour adds emphasis; the word carries the meaning.
- **Motion.** Animation is kept to interaction and active work. Yami's Reduced
  motion setting (Settings > Behavior) or the Windows reduced-motion preference
  stops the transitions described here. Nothing keeps moving while you only
  look at a screen.

## Light and dark on a schedule

Settings › System › **Light and dark on a schedule** switches Windows (apps and the system) to dark mode from an evening time and back to light from a morning time (default 19:00 and 07:00). A night schedule, with dark during the day, also works. Yami keeps one timer to the next switch. It applies the theme when you turn the schedule on or change the times, and at each switch, so a manual change in between stays until the next switch. It uses the same registry setting and broadcast as the Controls card's dark mode toggle. The rules are unit-tested; on 2026-10-28, with the owner's approval, `scripts/verify-theme-live.mjs` switched Windows to the other mode (the taskbar had repainted within six seconds) and back, writing the exact earlier values at the end. **Wallpaper rotation:** the Wallpaper panel's menu can change the wallpaper every hour or every day, moving through your Yami library in order and skipping pictures that have gone missing. One timer is set for the next change (no polling), the first change comes a full interval after you turn it on, and it needs at least two pictures. The timing and the next-picture rule are unit-tested with mock timers; the switch itself is the same native call the packaged check exercises. The accent tweak is under Tune › Look and feel.

## Feeds inbox

Turn on **Feeds inbox** in Settings › Modules (or the Developer preset) and Desk gains a **Plans | Inbox** switch. Add a subreddit (`r/rust`), Hacker News, a GitHub repository's releases (`owner/repo`), a YouTube channel ID, a Bluesky profile (`name.bsky.social`, read from its public `/rss`), a Mastodon account (`@user@instance`, read from `/@user.rss`) or any HTTPS RSS, Atom or JSON Feed address. Untitled social posts use their text as the title. Items from every source are listed newest first; a chip per source filters the list, shows its last error on hover, and removes it. Opening an item opens your browser.

Feeds are read when the Inbox opens, and only for sources not read in the last ten minutes, or when you press refresh. There is no background timer. Each read sends the saved ETag and Last-Modified, waits at most 15 seconds, accepts at most 2 MB, refuses a redirect to plain HTTP, keeps the previous items when a read fails, and stores up to 50 items per source in `feeds.json`. Titles and summaries are shown as plain text, and only http(s) links are kept. Up to 50 sources. On 2026-10-28, Hacker News, GitHub releases and Reddit's public `.rss` feeds all answered. On 2026-10-28 a Bluesky profile and a Mastodon account also answered; Reddit may answer HTTP 429 when it is read several times in a few minutes, which the Inbox shows as "asked Yami to slow down". **Mail** sits at the top of the Inbox: up to five accounts (Gmail, Fastmail, iCloud Mail, Yahoo Mail or any IMAP server on port 993). Each shows its unread and total count and the five newest senders and subjects, read when the Inbox opens. You sign in with an app password, which is checked once when you add the account and then kept with Yami's DPAPI-encrypted credentials, never in `mail.json`. Yami opens INBOX with `EXAMINE` and reads headers with `BODY.PEEK`, so nothing is marked as read, moved, deleted or sent, and no message body or image is loaded. The protocol is unit-tested against a scripted server, and a real Gmail server answered TLS and refused a fake sign-in on 2026-10-28; reading a **real mailbox is not verified**. **Outlook.com** signs in with Microsoft instead of a password: Yami starts Microsoft's device-code sign-in and shows a short code with **Open the page** (`microsoft.com/link`); once you enter it and agree, Microsoft gives Yami a token for `outlook.office365.com` IMAP (`AUTHENTICATE XOAUTH2`, scope `IMAP.AccessAsUser.All`). Yami never sees your password. The refresh token is kept with Yami's DPAPI-encrypted credentials, the short-lived access token only in memory, and each read refreshes it when due. Microsoft requires an app (client) ID: register a free app at entra.microsoft.com (personal Microsoft accounts, public client flows allowed) and paste its ID; it is saved as the `microsoftClientId` setting. The protocol and token flow are unit-tested against scripted servers; on 2026-10-29 Microsoft's real endpoint answered and refused a made-up app ID. Signing in to a real Outlook.com mailbox is not verified. **Brief me** sends the newest 40 headlines (source and title only, marked as untrusted data) to Gemini with your key and shows a short bulleted brief above the Inbox. It runs only when you press it, and without a key it asks for one. **Bluesky** in the Inbox shows your Following timeline after you sign in once with a Bluesky app password (`xxxx-xxxx-xxxx-xxxx`; a main password is refused before anything is sent). Yami calls `com.atproto.server.createSession` at `bsky.social`, keeps only the refresh token in the encrypted credentials, reads `app.bsky.feed.getTimeline` (30 posts) from your PDS when the Inbox opens or you press refresh, and renews an expired session by itself. Posts are plain text with author, time and repost note, and open on bsky.app. It cannot post, like or follow, and no images load. Unit-tested against a scripted server; on 2026-10-29 the real `bsky.social` refused a made-up app password as expected. A real signed-in timeline is not verified. Other home timelines that need sign-in (Mastodon) are not built.

## Yami Browser and Vault

Turn on **Yami Browser** in Settings › Modules, then open it from the launcher (type "browser"). It is Electron's own Chromium in one window, with a vertical tab rail, back, forward and reload, and an address bar that takes addresses, searches (DuckDuckGo) and bangs (`!g`, `!ddg`, `!b`, `!w`, `!yt`, `!gh`, `!npm`, `!r`, `!mdn`).
- **Isolation:** pages run in their own `persist:yami-browser` profile in Yami's data folder, sandboxed, with no access to Yami or Node.
- **No device access:** every camera, microphone, location and notification request is refused.
- **Web pages only:** only http and https pages load; `javascript:`, `file:` and other addresses become a search.
- **Tabs:** a link that opens a new window opens a new tab instead, up to twelve tabs. A tab left in the background for five minutes is closed to free memory and reloads when you return to it ("asleep" in the rail).

The **Vault** imports bookmarks from Chrome, Edge, Brave, Vivaldi or Helium on this PC, read from each browser's plain `Bookmarks` file, and keeps them encrypted with Windows DPAPI in `vault.bin`. The new-tab page lists and filters them. Yami never decrypts other browsers' passwords or cookies.

With **Yami for your agents** on, four browser tools (all off by default) let an agent work in the current tab while a bar says what it is doing: `browser_open` and `browser_read` open an address and read its title, address and visible text; `browser_click` presses a link or button found by its visible text or a CSS selector; and `browser_type` fills a field found by its label, placeholder or selector, without submitting. The page code runs in an isolated world the page's scripts cannot change. A click that submits a form or whose label says buy, pay, order, post, send, delete, subscribe or similar waits for Yami's approval card. Password fields are never typed into.

Checked on Windows on 2026-10-28 against a local test page: tabs, the page view's placement, background-tab sleep and wake, refusing `javascript:`, and an agent reading over MCP. **Linked browser mode.** Google, banks and some other sites refuse to sign in inside embedded browsers, and Yami does not disguise itself. So the new-tab page can open an installed Microsoft Edge, Google Chrome, Brave or Helium instead. It runs with its own Yami profile folder (never your usual profile) and a debugging port on `127.0.0.1:47824` only, so sign-ins work normally and `browser_read` reads that window while it is open. Closing it from Yami ends it. ## Yami Remote (phone)

Turn on **Yami Remote** in Settings › Phone and scan the pairing code with your phone's camera. Yami serves a small Nightglow page at `http://<this PC's home-network address>:47822/`, listening only on that private address and only while the module is on. The page shows agents that are working, and:
- **Approve agents** (on by default): a coding agent's permission request appears with **Deny** and **Allow once**, the same decision as the card on the PC.
- **Control music** (on by default): back, play/pause and next for what is playing on the PC.
- **Send to the clipboard** (off by default): text you send lands on the PC's clipboard, up to 10,000 characters.

Each ability has its own switch. The pairing code carries a token in the address's `#` part, which the phone keeps and removes from the address bar. Every request needs it, and **New pairing code** unpairs every phone. The page loads nothing from the internet, sets its text without HTML, and refreshes every four seconds only while it is visible. Yami Remote never shows the screen or controls the mouse, and it has no relay: away from home, use your own VPN such as Tailscale. Windows may ask once to let Yami through the firewall on private networks.

The server, its token and ability checks, and the phone page (pairing, an approval, HTML safety) were checked against a loopback address on 2026-10-28. Listening on the home network and a real phone were **not exercised**, to avoid a firewall prompt during unattended checks. **Screen view and control** replace a separate remote-desktop program: two more abilities, both off until you switch them on. **View the screen** lets the paired phone ask for a JPEG of this PC's main screen (at most 1,280 pixels wide), about once a second while its Screen section is open and the page is visible; nothing is captured otherwise, and Yami shows a notice on the PC whenever viewing starts again after a minute. **Control the PC** (it also needs View the screen) lets the phone tap to click, right-click, scroll, type text and press Enter, Backspace, Tab, Esc, Space or the arrow keys; anything else is refused before it reaches Windows. Input goes through Yami's native host as absolute positions on the main screen, so display scaling does not matter, and text is typed as Unicode keys without touching the clipboard. Windows does not let it control windows running as administrator. Checked on Windows on 2026-10-28 on loopback: off by default, a real frame of about 50 KB, control refused while only viewing is on, and an unknown key refused. On the same day the desktop check brought Yami's window to the front, confirmed with Windows that it was the foreground window before every input, and then, as the phone would, tapped Yami's chat box (it took focus), typed "hello from phone!" and pressed Backspace; the box read "hello from phone". Taps land exactly on the requested point (checked against the cursor position). A tap can be hidden by Yami's own notice toast, which appears when viewing or control starts. An earlier run without the foreground check sent one tap to the app that was in front. **Mail catcher** (Dev › Projects) is Yami's own small SMTP server for testing the mail an app sends; nothing is downloaded or installed for it. **Start** listens on `127.0.0.1:1025` only (not on your network, so Windows does not ask about the firewall), accepts any login, offers no TLS (turn TLS off in the app's settings) and relays nothing: every message stops in Yami. Caught mail is saved as `.eml` files in Yami's data folder, the newest 500 are kept, a message over 10 MB is refused, and the list refreshes as mail arrives. Opening one shows its sender, recipients, date and text, with HTML turned into plain text so no image, tracker or script from it ever loads, plus attachment names; **Open in mail app** hands the `.eml` to your mail program. **Stop** closes the port, and Yami stops it when it quits. Checked on Windows on 2026-10-28: started from the page, a message sent over SMTP from outside Yami appeared and opened as text, and the port refused connections after Stop. Yami runs no public mail server: port 25 is usually blocked, home IP addresses are on spam blocklists, and delivering mail needs DNS, PTR records and an always-on host.

## Tune (Windows tune-up)

Turn on **Windows tune-up** in Settings › Modules (or the Tinkerer preset) and a **Tune** tab joins the dock. It lists fourteen per-user tweaks in five groups:
- **Explorer:** file extensions, hidden files, open to This PC, the classic right-click menu.
- **Taskbar and Start:** icons on the left, End task in the taskbar menu, seconds on the clock, no web results in Start search, no suggested apps.
- **Privacy:** no advertising ID, no tailored experiences, no tips.
- **Gaming:** no background game recording.
- **Comfort:** no Sticky Keys prompt.

Every tweak writes only `HKEY_CURRENT_USER` values through `reg.exe`, so there is no administrator prompt. Before changing anything, Yami writes the exact earlier values to `tune.json` in its data folder. Turning a tweak off restores them, removes values that were not set, and deletes a key Yami created. A tweak that was already set before Yami shows **Already set** and stays locked, because there is nothing of Yami's to undo. Tweaks that Explorer reads at start offer **Restart Explorer**, which asks first and never runs on its own. The tweak list was adapted from the ideas in Chris Titus Tech's WinUtil (no code copied). **Look and feel** is Yami's own take on popular Explorer and taskbar mods, using settings Windows already reads instead of injecting code into Explorer: hide the taskbar search box, hide the Task View button, never combine taskbar buttons, compact File Explorer, the full path in the title bar, selection checkboxes, no recent files in Home, and no snap layouts on the maximize button. **Yami's violet as the Windows accent** writes the same per-user values Settings › Personalization › Colours writes (the accent palette, Start and menu colours, and the window frame colour) with a deeper violet from Yami's glow so white text stays readable; Explorer reads them after it restarts, which the page offers. Like every tweak they are applied and undone in a sandbox registry key by the desktop check (23 tweaks on 2026-10-28); the same live check applied it to the real registry and undid it, ending with the exact earlier values; Explorer was not restarted, so the change was confirmed in the registry rather than on screen (this PC does not show the accent on the taskbar). **Startup apps** lists what starts with Windows: your account's `Run` entries and Startup folder shortcuts, which you can switch off and on, and all-users entries, shown with **Needs admin** and not changed. Switching off works like Task Manager: Yami writes the `StartupApproved` entry (`03` and the time) and keeps the app's own entry; switching on writes `02`.**Mouse pointer** (Tune) offers the pointer schemes Windows lists in its Mouse settings (from `HKLM\…\Control Panel\Cursors\Schemes`, with Windows Aero shown as Windows Default, plus your own saved schemes) and the pointer size 1–15 from Settings › Accessibility. Picking one writes the same per-user values those pages write: the 17 pointer paths in `HKCU\Control Panel\Cursors` in Windows' order, the scheme name and `Scheme Source`, and `CursorSize` with `CursorBaseSize` (32 pixels plus 16 per step). Yami then asks Windows to reload the pointers (`SystemParametersInfo(SPI_SETCURSORS)` in the native host). Before its first change Yami saves the earlier values in `tune-cursors.json`; **Put back** writes them again exactly, removing values that were not set. The desktop check ran in a sandbox copy of your pointer values on 2026-10-29 and left the real ones unchanged; applying to the real pointer and the on-screen reload were not exercised.

 **Network DNS** lists each connected network adapter with its DNS servers, read without administrator rights. Pick Cloudflare, Cloudflare for Families, Quad9, AdGuard DNS, Google Public DNS, or Automatic (back to your router's DNS). Yami asks first, then Windows shows one administrator prompt. A short elevated PowerShell receives only the adapter's number and a profile name from Yami's fixed list, sets the servers and clears the DNS cache. Reading DNS and refusing bad input were checked on Windows on 2026-10-28; applying a change was exercised once with the owner's approval on the same day (see below). **Encrypt lookups** (shown under an adapter that uses one of those profiles) turns on Windows 11's own DNS over HTTPS: the same elevated script registers the profile's DoH address (`Add-`/`Set-DnsClientDohServerAddress`, auto-upgrade on, no fallback to plain UDP) and writes `DohFlags = 1` under the adapter's `Dnscache\InterfaceSpecificParameters\{GUID}\DohInterfaceSettings\Doh\<server>` key, as published Windows 11 guides describe (Microsoft does not document these per-adapter values). ## Desk widget

**Desk widget** (a module, off by default; in the Tinkerer and Everything presets) puts one small card on the desktop: the time and date, today's weather when the Weather module is on, what is playing with play/pause and next, and up to two active agents when the AI workspace is on, the most urgent first; otherwise it says "All quiet." ## Known boundaries

- Windows sleep wake, microphone permissions, brightness, radio toggles, media sessions, and app discovery are affected by system state or hardware.
- Yami cannot act on the secure lock/UAC desktop or after a full shutdown.
- Plans uses the Windows date-and-time control (`datetime-local`). Its icon is tinted to match Yami, but the picker itself is still the Windows one.
- Clipboard and private-window exclusions are heuristics, not a universal privacy detector.
- The app does not send email, chat messages, or make purchases through its model tools. These are explicitly forbidden by the prompt and no such declared action exists.
- Terminal and coordinate tools are disabled by default. If enabled, they act with the signed-in Windows user’s privileges.
